Diego Serrano Ortega

I am a PhD candidate in Statistics for Data Science at the Department of Statistics of Universidad Carlos III de Madrid, and a member of the NICDA group (Nonparametric Inference for Complex Data and its Applications). My thesis, Topics on random forests in metric spaces, is supervised by Eduardo García-Portugués and Marco Raiola.

I come from mathematics: a BSc at Universidad Autónoma de Madrid, then the MSc in Statistics for Data Science at UC3M, and research work since October 2023.

Research

I work on inference for random objects: data valued in a general metric space rather than a Euclidean one — distributions, covariance matrices, curves, directions, shapes. Only a distance is assumed, so the tools apply across very different kinds of data, at the price of rebuilding notions as basic as the mean. The driving application is aeroacoustics: the flow fields and acoustic curves measured from turbulent jets lose information when forced into a Euclidean representation.

Two lines so far: out-of-bag prediction balls, uncertainty regions for the prediction of any bagging regression model with metric-space responses, with asymptotic coverage guarantees; and, with Ingrid Van Keilegom (KU Leuven) and Eduardo García-Portugués, a goodness-of-fit framework for distributions on separable metric spaces built on distance profiles.
