Diego Serrano Ortega

I am a PhD candidate in Statistics for Data Science at the Department of Statistics of Universidad Carlos III de Madrid, and a member of the NICDA group (Nonparametric Inference for Complex Data and its Applications). My thesis, Topics on random forests in metric spaces, is supervised by Eduardo García-Portugués and Marco Raiola.

My background is in mathematics. During my BSc at Universidad Autónoma de Madrid I grew interested in probability, statistical inference and scientific computing, and I wrote a final-year thesis on continued fractions. Looking for problems where mathematical structure meets data, I moved to the MSc in Statistics for Data Science at UC3M. I began doing research in October 2023 as a research technician working on reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and support vector machines, and started my doctorate in November 2024.

Research

I develop inferential tools for random objects: data taking values in a general metric space rather than a Euclidean one — probability distributions, covariance matrices, curves, directions, shapes. The framework asks for no algebraic structure and no local Euclideanity, only a distance, so the methods apply across very different kinds of data. The price is that familiar notions such as the mean or the variance have to be rebuilt from scratch, and much of the inferential toolbox is still missing. My thesis tries to fill in some of it.

The driving application is aeroacoustics. Reducing the noise emitted by commercial aircraft, a priority for the European aviation sector, requires understanding how the turbulent structures in the mixing layer of a jet generate the sound it radiates. The data involved — vectorial flow fields measured by particle image velocimetry, acoustic curves recorded by microphone arrays — can be Euclideanised through principal component projections, but only at the cost of losing information. Treating them as elements of a metric space is the more faithful option.

Two lines of work so far. The first introduced out-of-bag prediction balls: uncertainty regions for the prediction of any bagging regression model with responses in a metric space, with asymptotic coverage guarantees. The second, developed with Ingrid Van Keilegom (KU Leuven) and Eduardo García-Portugués, is a general goodness-of-fit framework for distributions on separable metric spaces. Goodness of fit has largely been addressed one geometry at a time, through tests tailored to particular spaces or parametric families; we instead build on distance profiles, covering simple and composite null hypotheses, and establish the asymptotic null distribution of the test statistics along with their consistency against fixed alternatives and their weak limits under local ones.
